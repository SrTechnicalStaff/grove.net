01Default is full widthMH-00: one open slate takes the whole strip. Half width exists only when a second peer is assigned — never as a default.
02Camera-blindFixed to the viewport. Panning or zooming changes the scene behind; the slate never moves, scales, or reflows with the camera.
03The Grid never resizesOpening a slate overlays the Grid; it does not squeeze it into a smaller viewport. Closing restores nothing, because nothing moved.
04Host commandsAdd assigns a second slate; Close returns to the invoking surface. Product verbs only, in the header — no chrome elsewhere.

01Explicit entryMH-01: Add is the only way a second slate arrives. Nothing is auto-assigned, nothing appears on hover.
02Three placementsLeft · Right · Full is the entire choice. Menu class anatomy: label left in UI type, placement glyph right.
03Occupancy is shownThe glyph marks where the new slate lands (interaction) against what already sits there (slate hue). No blind commit.
04Divider previewThe resulting split is previewed before commit — the occupied side keeps its name; the new side reads as arriving.

01Peers, not primary/secondaryDeliberate divergence from HIG split view: neither pane owns the other. No master list drives a detail view; the gap between them shows live Grid context.
02State is per paneIdentity, scroll, selection, filter, draft, playback — each pane keeps its own. Nothing is shared or proxied across the divider.
03Focus is structuralActive pane: interaction border and full header ink. Inactive: hairline and reduced ink. A grayscale read still finds focus.
04Selection stays localInteraction outline outside the frame, in the active pane only. The image itself is never restyled.
05Command routingAssignment and close belong to the host; scroll, selection, and content verbs go to the active pane. One rule, no exceptions.

01Rail, not removalMH-03: below the two-pane minimum, one pane expands and the other parks as a rail. Scroll, selection, filters, and drafts all survive there.
02Full measure returnsThe expanded pane gets the whole width — no squeezed columns, no truncation to keep both visible.

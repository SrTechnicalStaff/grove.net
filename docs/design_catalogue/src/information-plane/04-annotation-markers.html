AM-00 · QUIET FIELD
READING MARKERS · NOTES ON 0102 / 18
01Quiet is the defaultA blip appears only where gathered material has already passed the gate. The absence of a marker is information: nothing here is worth opening yet.
02Cells, not cloudsThe field is painted one cell at a time with hard edges, because the measure behind it is a per-cell fact. A soft glow would promise a precision the measure does not have.
03The perimeter is the groupCells above the contour draw a 1px edge only where the neighbouring cell falls outside the set. That outline is the shape of what a marker would gather.
04Below the gate, nothing happensTwo scattered pieces make a field but not a reading. Grove neither invents a marker to reward proximity nor shows the numbers it used to decide.
05Presence survives distanceEvery placement holds its true position in a simpler form: a Note keeps its colour, a Document keeps paper, an Image keeps its plate. Distance removes chrome, then detail — never presence.
06The field takes the material's own hueCells carry the mixed hue of the sources feeding them: neutral for Documents and Images, the Note's own colour for Notes, Anchor indigo where authored context exists. Annotation invents no hue of its own.
07A second concentration is a second candidateSeparated groups qualify on their own evidence and carry their own marker. One reading per group; neighbours never merge into one.
08Depth is part of the fieldPlacements on nearby Layers contribute through the same falloff, so a group can be built across Layers without the drawing pretending they sit on one.

AM-00 · WHOLE-AREA AND LOCAL CANDIDATES
READING MARKERS · NOTES ON 0405 / 18
01Two tiers, one grammarA whole-area candidate covers everything one concentration holds. A local candidate covers one dense group inside it. They differ in what they gather, never in how they look.
02One marker per groupGroups are found first, then exactly one marker is assigned to each. Without that rule a busy area would sprout a marker for every pair of pieces.
03The dense group keeps its ownA tight group inside a larger area is still worth reading on its own terms, so it carries a marker as well as the area around it.
04Separation makes a neighbourConcentrations held apart by relative distance stay separate candidates — neighbouring systems inside one galaxy, not one body.
05Groups are found by overlapPieces join when their fields overlap on the same target Layer. Sitting next to each other is not a relationship, and guessing the topic is not used at all.
06Quiet constructionA 1px interaction ring at 0.7, fill at 0.2, a solid core inset 3px, and a soft halo. Enough to notice at rest; nothing that pulses for attention.
07No counts, no scores, no badgesThe blip carries no number and no saturation value. What it gathered is answered by engaging it, not by decorating it.
08The blip does not scaleThe marker belongs to the reading layer, not the Grid. It keeps its size and its place as the view moves, cannot be selected, and never becomes a placed object.

AM-01 · ACTIVE MARKER · ANCHOR → TITLE → IMAGE → TEXT
READING MARKERS · NOTES ON 0607 / 18
01Proximity engages, not aimThe pointer never has to hit the dot. Resting over a piece the marker gathered engages it, because the cue is for material the person is already near.
02The concentration engages tooThe dense cells the marker was built from are part of its reach. Hovering the area works even where nothing is placed.
03Hover engages, choosing opensEngagement is free and reversible; opening the reading is deliberate. Escape returns to the quiet state and touches neither the Grid nor the view.
04One active at a timeMoving to another candidate replaces the active one, and its neighbours stay quiet blips. Cues never accumulate on screen.
05Anchor firstIf a gathered piece carries authored context, that context is the cue, marked in Anchor indigo. A person's own words about their material outrank anything Grove could derive.
06Then a titleOtherwise the title of the nearest piece on the same Layer names the group — the closest thing to a name the material already has.
07Then the pictureWith no Anchor and no title, an Image shows a thumbnail beside its name, so a visual group stays visually identifiable.
08Then the words themselvesLast, a short passage from the nearest text, set in reading type and wrapped. A sentence is not a title: it is never set in display caps and never cut off with an ellipsis.
09One action, no menuThe cue answers "what is here" and offers a single way in. Destinations for individual pieces belong to the reading, where each piece can be named.

GROVE — ANNOTATIONS · STAIRCASE LINE, NO ANCHORS
READING MARKERS · NOTES ON 0809 / 18
01The line starts at the centre of the materialIts foot sits where the gathered fields overlap most; ties break toward the smallest distance between the pieces themselves. The cue points at the material's centre of gravity, not at a cell someone chose.
02A staircase, not a leader lineSteps read as a route to somewhere rather than a pin stuck into the Grid. The line carries no anchors, no arrowhead, and no terminal dot.
03The cue sits at the head of the routeThe card is the destination of the line, so the connection between what is drawn and what is named needs no label to explain it.
04It overlaps the field it belongs toDrawn over its own concentration, the line says which material the cue is about without covering the pieces it gathered.
05Only one route is drawnThe other candidates stay as blips. Two staircases at once would turn the Grid into a diagram of itself.
06Motion is courtesy, not messageThe line draws on approach and settles. With reduced motion it simply appears at the same threshold, and the settled frame carries exactly the same meaning.
07Nothing here is a Grid objectLine, blip, and cue live on the reading layer. They occupy no cells, join no selection, enter no history — and the view is never re-framed to show them.

AM-02 / AR-00…AR-04 · LOCAL READING BESIDE ITS SOURCE
READING MARKERS · NOTES ON 1011 / 18
01It opens beside its sourceThe reading is framed next to the material it was made from, and that material stays visible. It is not centred, and nothing behind it is dimmed.
02Escape is always the way backEscape closes this reading only, and focus returns to whatever invoked it. Closing a destination comes back here while its route is still valid.
03The page is paperEditions read as print: paper fill, ink text, a readable measure, steady rhythm. In Grove a light surface always means a page, never chrome.
04Every piece keeps its formNotes and Documents stay text; Images and GIFs keep complete frames at their own proportions. Nothing is flattened into one generic card or re-encoded to fit.
05Footer notes name the originEach piece carries the Layer it came from and how many placements it has, the way a magazine keeps its running credits. Provenance is part of the reading.
06Four named destinationsGrid placement returns to where the piece was put; Core Memory opens the record itself; Writing Slate takes text; Image Viewer takes pictures. Grove never infers a destination from the form.
07Pages, never cutsWhen material exceeds the page it continues onto the next one. Nothing is truncated, summarized, or shrunk below the readable measure to make a page fit.
08Paging is the whole navigationPage context stays in quiet monospace at the foot. There is no scrollbar race, no zoom control, and no second reading opened alongside.
09Reading changes nothingOpening, paging, and returning create no Memory, move no Placement, write no history — and never re-centre the view. The source stays exactly where the person put it.

APN-01 / APN-02 · AM-03 / AM-04 · ONE PINNED READING
READING MARKERS · NOTES ON 1213 / 18
01The source stays in view beside itA pinned reading is still a local one. It sits next to the material it was made from, and that material is never covered or moved to make room.
02Pin is a deliberate keepAn active reading follows engagement and disappears with it. Pinning says: hold this one while I look around. It is not a promotion to a fixed panel at the edge of the screen.
03The action states its own stateThe control reads Pin reading before and Pinned after, taking the interaction fill so the kept reading is legible at a glance. One reading is kept at a time; a second request is answered in words, never by a silent swap.
04The return marker appears only when neededWhile the source is in view the reading speaks for itself. When the source leaves view, one restrained marker remains — and choosing it restores the same page, the same piece, and the same routes back to source.
05The pinned reading does not follow the viewIt stays with its material rather than riding along at a fixed screen position. Moving away is how the person left it; it is not how they lose it.
06Returning does not move the viewThe reading comes back to the person. The Grid context is never re-framed, no placement is disturbed, and nothing is selected on the way.
07A pin is not a recordPinned state lasts the session. It creates no Memory, changes no Placement, and leaves nothing behind to clean up. Dismissing releases the pin and returns focus.

AM-02 RECOVERY · APN-03 · T-AN08 REFUSAL
READING MARKERS · NOTES ON 1415 / 18
01A second pin is refused in wordsWith one pin available, asking for another explains the limit instead of quietly replacing the reading the person chose to keep. The wording says what to do next rather than what went wrong.
02Refusal wears the invalid role, and nothing else doesOnly the pin control and its explanation change; the reading itself stays open and fully usable. Refusal is not an error state and never becomes a floating alert.
03Stale is explained, not hiddenWhen the gathered material changes underneath a reading, the reading says so and offers the way back. It does not rearrange itself without warning or vanish while the person is reading it.
04An unavailable piece keeps its placeA source that cannot be shown stays in the page with its name, its origin, and an invalid edge. Dropping it would quietly rewrite what was gathered.
05One piece failing does not fail the pageThe rest of the reading continues around it, in order. Only the frame that cannot be shown is marked.
06The original is always safe — and says soRecovery wording answers the person's first question directly, because a missing frame inside a reading looks exactly like lost work.
07The failure is named where it happenedExplanations live inline in the reading that failed, beside the piece that failed. Grove has no dialog box, no scrim, and no floating confirmation.

DESIGN.MD OVERLAYS & CHROME · READING SURFACES
READING MARKERS · REFUSALS18 / 18
×No marker without spatial evidenceQualification comes from overlapping fields on a target Layer and measured source mass. Grove never groups material by guessing what it is about, and never rewards mere adjacency.
×No measurements in the interfaceSupported cells, saturation, and source mass are engineering evidence. The person sees a marker or nothing at all.
×No view movement, everApproaching, opening, pinning, and returning never re-centre or zoom. A reading that moved the view would take the person's place from them.
×No qualification that depends on the viewThe same material qualifies at any zoom or pan. Otherwise a marker would blink in and out as the person looked around.
×No second active, no second pinOne marker is active and one reading is kept. A second pin is refused with an explanation; it is never a silent swap.
×No centred panel and no dimmingThe reading is local to its material or it is not a reading. There is no scrim, no dialog, and no floating confirmation anywhere in this journey.
×No truncation, no summary, no shrinkingPages are the only answer to material that does not fit. Nothing is cut, condensed, or reduced below a readable measure — including the cue.
×No writing of any kindA reading creates no Memory, no Placement, and no history entry. Pinned state ends with the session, and an excerpt never becomes new material by itself.
×No dropped sourceA piece that cannot be shown keeps its place and its provenance. Silently removing it would rewrite what the person actually gathered.
×No unsupported material pretending to workNotes, Documents, Images, and GIFs are the first reading. Audio, video, PDF, and EPUB are absent rather than half-shown.
×No marker as a placed objectBlips, staircases, and cues occupy no cells, join no selection, and cannot be moved, copied, or traced. They are not content.
×No system vocabulary in a cueMarkers and readings name the material and the destination. Nothing on screen explains Grove to itself.

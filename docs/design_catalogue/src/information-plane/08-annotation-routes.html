AS-00 · SOURCE ITEM SELECTED
SOURCE ROUTES · NOTES ON 0101 / 09
01The reading stays putThe menu opens over the page it belongs to. Nothing dims, nothing slides in from an edge, and the page behind stays fully readable. Opening a menu does nothing at all; only choosing a row acts.
02Routes belong to a pieceA route is offered by one item, never by the reading as a whole. The chosen item takes the interaction outline outside its frame, so the printed page is never restyled to show what is selected.
03Label left, one key rightEach row is one line: plain words on the left, a single quick key in monospace at reduced ink on the right. The keys match what the same piece offers elsewhere, so nothing has to be relearned inside a reading.
04Open it, or find itThe hairline separates the two questions a reader actually asks: open this piece to work on it, or find where this piece lives. Merging them into one action is what made the old confusion between a record and its place on the Grid.
05The same cloth as every menuSurface fill, one quiet 1px border, 2px radius, no scrim, no shadow beyond soft separation. A reading is not a special case: this is the row grammar used everywhere else in Grove.
06The page already says where it came fromEvery item prints its Layer and how many placements it has, set like a magazine footer note. Provenance is part of the reading, so the menu never has to explain itself.
07One quiet line for answersThe line under the title is where the reading speaks: how many pieces it gathered, that it is pinned, or that a route could not be taken. Grove has no dialog box, so there is nowhere else for a refusal to appear.

AN09-A1 · D-ANNOTATION-01 · D-CM-01
SOURCE ROUTES · NOTES ON 0202 / 09
01Text goes to the writerA Note or a Document offers Writing Slate, and it says so by name because that is where sustained writing happens in Grove. A reading is for reading; it is never turned into an editor.
02Pictures go to the viewerAn Image offers Open, which is what opening a picture already means everywhere else — the viewer, at full fidelity. A GIF is an Image for this purpose: same row, same key, playback is the viewer's business.
03Two rows every piece carriesWhere it sits and what it is are always available: Show on the Grid for the place, Open record for the piece itself. They are separate rows because they answer separate questions and neither implies the other.
04Never placed, so never pretendedA piece with no place on the Grid cannot be shown on it. Instead of a dead row, the menu offers the ordinary way to place it, and placement stays the deliberate act it is everywhere else.
05No rows, no menuA piece that can no longer be resolved keeps its frame, its title, and its footer note, and says plainly that the original is safe. It offers no menu at all, because an empty menu is an apology, not an action.
06The page already said soThe footer note prints not placed, so an absent row is explained before it can be missed. What a piece is decides which rows exist; it never decides which one is taken. Reading, editing, retrieving, and revisiting are four different intents.

AS-01 · AN09-A2
SOURCE ROUTES · NOTES ON 0303 / 09
01Asked for by nameThis is the only route allowed to move the view, and only because a person chose the row that says so. Every other route leaves the view exactly where it was. Nothing about a reading is ever a reason for the Grid to move on its own.
02Framed, not fetchedArriving frames the piece on its own Layer through the same framing the Grid already uses, and leaves it selected. The piece is not moved, re-placed, copied, or altered; a placement is a fact of the Grid, and the reading only points at it.
03The reading does not followA reading lives beside its source, so when the view travels the reading does not come along. Pinned first, it waits: a restrained marker names the saved reading and takes you back to it, unchanged, whenever you want it.
04Nothing to show, so nothing shownA piece that has never been placed offers the ordinary place action instead. This is the same handoff Grove uses everywhere, entered from the reading rather than invented by it.
05A footprint before a commitmentPlacing shows the footprint and whether it fits, and waits. A route may start that handoff; it may never finish one silently. No route has ever created a placement by itself.
06Everything else stands stillOther pieces keep their cells, other Layers keep their order, and no record is written by traveling. The one visible change is where you are looking.

AS-02 · CORE MEMORY ROUTE
SOURCE ROUTES · NOTES ON 0404 / 09
01The record surface, not a popupOpen record lands in Memory Slate: opaque fill, one quiet border, generous padding, fixed to the viewport. It does not dim the Grid behind it, and it does not float free of the edge it is docked to.
02The piece itselfWhat opens is the original — its current state, its dates, its history — not the copy that happened to appear in the reading. This is the route for "what is this, really", and it answers with the durable thing.
03Places are facts about it, not part of itHow many times a piece sits on the Grid is quiet metadata beside the record. The record opens the same way whether the piece is placed once, placed three times, or never placed at all.
04Not a lookup on the GridOpening a record does not frame anything, select anything, or move the view. Wanting to know what a piece is and wanting to see where it sits are two questions, and only the second one travels.
05The reading is still underneathThe reading is not closed, replaced, or rebuilt. Close the record and you are on the same page, with the same piece still chosen.
06Straight to the pieceThe route arrives at the record itself rather than dropping you into a gallery to search for it again. Browsing everything is what the surface is for on its own terms; this route already knows which piece you meant.

AS-03 · FORM ROUTES
SOURCE ROUTES · NOTES ON 0505 / 09
01Writing happens where writing happensA Note or Document opens in Writing Slate: docked to the viewport, generous, quiet, built for a long sitting. It carries the piece's name and Layer so there is never a doubt about which one is open.
02The destination keeps its own rulesDrafts, saving, and discarding belong to the writing surface and behave exactly as they do when it is opened any other way. The route only walks you in and walks you back; it never brings its own save button.
03A picture opens beside its pageAn Image or GIF opens in the viewer: a compact frame with the View border, its controls, and its own Back. Full fidelity, complete frame, no crop — the same viewer the picture would open in from the Grid.
04Above the reading, not instead of itThe viewer sits over the reading and the reading stays open underneath. One Escape closes the viewer and only the viewer; a second closes the reading. Nothing is stacked that cannot be unstacked one layer at a time.
05Same piece, different jobReading a piece in place, working on its text, and inspecting its full resolution are three intents. The reading offers all of them and assumes none: what a piece is never decides what you wanted to do with it.
06Neither one moves the viewOpening the writer or the viewer leaves the Grid exactly as it was — same position, same selection, same Layers. Only Show on the Grid travels, and only when it is chosen.

AS-03 · AN09-A3 · APN-01
SOURCE ROUTES · NOTES ON 0606 / 09
01Leaving is remembered, not reconstructedThe page you were on, the piece you chose, and the reading you came from are held by the trip itself. The destination is never asked to remember where you came from, so no destination can get it wrong.
02One way out, always visibleClose, Back, or Escape — the destination's own exit, no different from closing it any other way. Escape always dismisses the top surface first and only that one.
03The same reading, not another oneYou come back to page two of three with the same piece still chosen, not to a rebuilt reading opened at the beginning. A second reading of the same material is never created by leaving and coming back.
04Focus lands where it leftAttention returns to the piece that sent you, ready for the next key. A returning reader should be able to pick up mid-thought without hunting for their place.
05Pin is what survives a long detourAn unpinned reading is a passing thing; a pinned one waits beside its source with its page and its chosen piece intact, however far the view wanders in between.
06The limits do not reset by travelingOne reading open and one reading kept — the same limits on return as before departure. Coming back is not a loophole for a second pinned reading.

AS-04 · AN09-A4 · APN-03
SOURCE ROUTES · NOTES ON 0707 / 09
01The reading answers for itselfA route that cannot be taken says so on the reading's own quiet line — no dialog, no banner across the Grid, nothing to dismiss before reading can continue. Grove has no error box because it has no dialog box.
02Readable through the whole failureThe page keeps its pieces, its order, its provenance, and its page count. A failed route costs a sentence, never the reading.
03A missing piece keeps its placeMaterial that has gone stays in the sequence with its title and its footer note, marked in the invalid role, and says the original is safe. Dropping it silently would quietly rewrite what the reading was made of.
04Wrong door for this pieceA destination that does not fit the form refuses in the same voice. Rows are built from what a piece is, so this is the guard for a piece that changed underneath an open menu, not a normal outcome.
05A destination that would not openWhen a surface refuses the handoff, the refusal is reported here rather than left as a dead click. Nothing partial is left open, and nothing is written on the way out.
06The piece behind the item is goneThe broadest case: the original can no longer be found at all, so every route it offered is unavailable at once. The wording never blames the reader or names machinery.
07A kept reading fails out loudA pinned reading whose material disappears turns its own keep action to the invalid role and offers the only honest next step: close it and release what was kept. It never vanishes on its own.

SOURCE ROUTES · THE ROUTE SET08 / 09
ROW OFFERED BY ARRIVES AT OWNS FOCUS MOVES VIEW WRITES
Open in Writing SlateW NOTE · DOCUMENT WRITING SLATE HUD, VIEWPORT-FIXED NO ONLY THE SLATE'S OWN DRAFT RULES
OpenO IMAGE · GIF IMAGE VIEWER INFORMATION, LOCAL, ABOVE THE READING NO NOTHING
Show on the GridG ANY PLACED PIECE THE PLACEMENT ON ITS LAYER GRID YES — ON EXPLICIT CHOICE ONLY NOTHING; SELECTS, NEVER MOVES
PlaceP ANY UNPLACED PIECE THE EXISTING PLACEMENT HANDOFF GRID THE HANDOFF'S OWN FRAMING ONLY ON THE PERSON'S COMMIT
Open recordM EVERY SUPPORTED FORM MEMORY SLATE, ON THE RECORD HUD, VIEWPORT-FIXED NO NOTHING
01Nothing is created by going somewhereNo route creates a record, creates a placement, edits a piece, or makes a second reading. Travel is not authorship.
02One route may move the viewOnly Show on the Grid, and only because it was chosen by name. A record or a viewer never repositions the Grid.
03Every exit lands back hereClosing a destination returns to the reading that sent you, with its page and chosen piece intact, or explains why it cannot.
04A reading does not edit its materialRows open, show, and retrieve. Changing a piece happens in the surface that owns it, so a reading can never rewrite what it was made from.
05The supported forms are fourNotes and Documents as text; Images and GIFs as pictures. Anything else keeps its place in the reading and offers no route it cannot honor.
06One open, one keptThe limits on readings hold across every departure and every return, and a refused pin says so rather than replacing what is already kept.

SOURCE ROUTES · REFUSALS09 / 09
Four route designs Grove refuses, and the reason each one fails.
CHOSEN FOR YOU
Harbor at dusk
Opening viewer…
No route runs itselfSelecting a picture must not open a viewer, and selecting text must not open a writer. What a piece is decides which rows exist, never which one is taken. Reading it, editing it, and finding it are different wants.
GLYPH TOOLBAR
River prints
Eleven prints back from the lab.
Rows, not symbolsFour unlabeled squares cannot distinguish a place from a record from a writer. Routes are named in words with one quick key, because the difference between these destinations is the entire point of having them.
READING REPLACED
Writing
Eleven prints back from the lab.
A destination is a detour, not a swapClosing the writer here leaves nothing to come back to: the page, the position, and the pinned reading are gone. The reading stays open behind every destination so the return is always real.
CENTERED AND DIMMED
OpenO
Open recordM
Nothing floats free of what it servesA route menu belongs at the piece it acts on, at the pointer, flipping to stay on screen. Centering it and dimming the Grid hides the very material the choice is about — and the soft corners are not Grove's.

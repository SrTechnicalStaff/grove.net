# Product Outcome: Passive Awareness Of Surrounding Work

> **Outcome Statement**: 
> "I want to sense where heavy activity or related material exists across different parts of my workspace without having to search through every section manually."

---

## 1. Human Behavior Shift

### Previous Behavior (Status Quo)
In multi-layer or multi-page systems, work on hidden pages remains completely invisible until the user manually clicks through tabs or toggles layers on and off. Users frequently forget about related work on secondary planes or accidentally duplicate work because they cannot perceive ambient activity happening outside their immediate view.

### Transformed Behavior
Users working on one plane naturally perceive subtle background glows and color bleeding originating from work on adjacent planes. Like light filtering through stacked sheets of paper, high-density work on Layer 2 casts an aura glow onto Layer 1. Users immediately sense where activity and content reside across their workspace without flipping between planes.

---

## 2. Underlying Product Architecture & Primitives

This capability is built upon:

- **[Aura](../definitions/Aura.md)**: Emits energy that bleeds vertically across stacked spatial layers, combining hues into a composite visual field.
- **[Layer](../definitions/Layer.md)**: Organizes Z-plane levels while remaining vertically permeable to aura field radiation.
- **[Grid](../definitions/Grid.md)**: Stores field metadata in its cell Field Ledger, tracking contributing source content across all stacked planes.

---

## 3. Behavioral Verification Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Sensing Activity on Parallel Planes
- **Given** a user is working on Layer 1 of a spatial grid,
- **When** substantial content is added or modified on Layer 2 directly above the current spatial area,
- **Then** a subtle aura glow bleeds onto Layer 1, alerting the user to the presence of content on Layer 2.

### Scenario 2: Identifying Dense Cross-Layer Clusters
- **Given** a workspace contains multiple stacked layers with overlapping content,
- **When** the user pans across the canvas,
- **Then** regions with high multi-layer concentration display richer composite hues, signaling areas of heavy synthesis.
